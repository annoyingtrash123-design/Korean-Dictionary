## Systematic issues (both levels)

1. Overlapping homonyms were taught by spelling, not by sense: 5급 -는다니1 (명령 내용 질문 = "did they tell you to…?")
   was drafted as the connective "hearing that, I feel"; 4급 -는다니2 (의문) borrowed the echo question of 4급 -다니1.
   Read the curriculum variants column too: “-으라니2, -자니2” pointed straight at the reported-command question.
2. Invented contrasts between near-synonyms again (-느니/-을 바에는, -는 듯하다/-는 모양이다, 이며/이라든가): when a
   dictionary gives no difference, say they are close and give only structural differences.
3. Restrictions stated as absolute that are not: -고 해서 "no suggestion after it", -기 쉽다 "never about one event",
   -게 생겼다 "no positive uses", -게5 quiz "past tense only", -거라 "limited to certain verbs", -나 싶다 "adjectives
   take only -ㄴ가". Before writing "cannot", try a common counter-example.
4. "Wrong" sentences that are grammatical (의사는 동시에 작가이다, 작년에 비교해) or "right" sentences that repeat the
   error the note forbids (-은 나머지: 시험이 끝난 나머지). Each wrong/right pair should differ in exactly the point the
   note names.
5. English: reversed logic with 은/는 말할 것도 없고 (twice), tense slips (-을래야, -으므로, -고도), invented subjects
   ("we", "they"), dropped emphasis (오늘따라/그날따라, 마저 "even"). Translate the Korean, not the gist.
6. Collocations that are not natural with the auxiliary (말리고 들다, 덤비고 들다, 해 냈다 for 해냈다, 회의하는 가운데,
   경제 상황인 가운데): check -고 들다 / -어 내다 / -는 가운데 examples against krdict or a corpus.
7. Register labels: name the level (-데 하게체, -는다니2 아주낮춤 with no 요 form) rather than "informal"; and say when a
   colloquial form is not the standard spelling (-을래야 vs. -으려야).
